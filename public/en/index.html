The frustrating part is not that you understand nothing.
The frustrating part is precisely that you understand so much, and it still happens.

Does this sound familiar?

You wanted to respond differently. And yet the old pattern took over.

At work

What you intended

This week I’m not taking on anything extra.

What happened

Your manager asks who will take on the project. There’s a silence. You feel restlessness in your chest, your breath catches for a moment. ‘I’ll do it.’ You hear yourself say it before you’ve decided.

And afterwards

That evening you’re angry with yourself. And now you have to do it too.

In your relationship

What you intended

Tonight I’ll calmly tell him I felt alone.

What happened

You have the words ready. He sighs and looks at his phone. And suddenly you hear yourself getting sharp. Or you say ‘never mind’ and walk out of the room.

And afterwards

You lie awake. Why can’t I just say what I feel?

In a conversation

What you intended

I’ll listen first, and respond afterwards.

What happened

A colleague says: ‘Can I give you some feedback?’ Your cheeks get warm. You start explaining, justifying, defending. You don’t even hear what she actually said.

And afterwards

Afterwards you know exactly what you would rather have said.

With family

What you intended

I won’t get dragged in this time.

What happened

At the birthday party your mother makes that one remark. Within a second you feel twelve again. You adapt, and smile.

And afterwards

You drive home with a knot in your stomach.

You know where it comes from. You’ve explored it, felt it, talked it through. And yet it happens. It’s not a matter of too little insight.

Imagine

That same moment. But now it unfolds differently.

Staying with yourself

He sighs and picks up his phone. You notice it. And you stay with yourself. ‘I want to tell you something. Will you look at me for a moment?’ And then you say what you felt.

Setting your boundary

The silence falls after the question of who will take it on. You feel it, and your body stays calm. ‘Not this time, my week is full.’ No explanation. No guilt afterwards.

Listening without defending

‘Can I give you some feedback?’ You feel curious instead of tense. ‘What exactly do you mean?’ And you hear the whole story.

Not because you’ve got yourself better under control. But because the old pattern no longer decides first. How would that feel? And what would you do with all that space?

A different perspective

Knowing where your pattern comes from is different from knowing what sets it off now.

We grew up with one way of looking at patterns: through your history. That way isn’t wrong. It just doesn’t look at the place where the pattern gets switched on.

Your story does not switch on your pattern. Your neurobiology does, using a few fragments of your story. Your survival system wants to protect you. And it uses old information for that, information that hasn’t been updated yet.

That’s not your fault. And it can be changed. At the source.
