What is it?

What is the Trigger Breakfree Challenge?

A week in which you don’t try to understand your pattern even better. You work with it yourself.

You choose one pattern you really want to be free of. Saying yes when you mean no. Snapping. Shutting down. Working harder. Explaining yourself. And in seven days you discover what it protects you from, what sets it off for you, and what it’s like when it no longer starts automatically.

A short video from me every day. One thing to do every day, in your own week, in your own life. And a community of people who recognise this, where you share what you notice. I’m there with you.

You know this

You knew. You had promised yourself. And yet.

A remark. A particular tone of voice. Someone pulling away. A silence that lasts a moment too long. And before you know it, the very thing you thought you had moved beyond happens again. You adapt. You withdraw. You start pleasing others. You become sharp. You work harder. Or you begin to explain yourself.

Only afterwards do you think: damn. I’m doing it again.

Then the search starts all over. What haven’t I seen yet? Do I need to go further back? Have I not felt it thoroughly enough?

You can explain exactly where your pattern comes from. But that does not stop it from happening again.

What happens now

Imagine

The same moment, a different experience

There’s a silence in the meeting. ‘I’ll do it.’ It’s out before you’ve decided.

The same silence. Your body stays calm. You choose for yourself whether to take something on.

Your partner says something in that one tone of voice. You snap, or you withdraw.

You hear the tone. You stay with yourself, and say what you actually meant.

A colleague gives you feedback. You start explaining, defending, justifying.

You hear what is being said, without the old reaction taking over the conversation.

Afterwards you know exactly what you would rather have done. But in the moment, that choice didn’t seem to be there.

The choice is simply there. Nothing spectacular. Often perfectly ordinary.

After a setback, the self-blame begins. ‘See? It’s me.’

A setback is information. ‘Hey, interesting. My system is still protecting me from something.’

Why insight is not enough

Knowing where it comes from doesn’t change what sets it off now.

Your story helps you understand what has happened in your life. Your neurobiology determines how old information still plays an automatic role today. Your survival system uses a few fragments of your story to protect you. Faster than you can think. That’s why all your understanding doesn’t help you in the moment.

How ROOTS began

One question I could never let go of.

In my early years in this work, I saw something that kept bothering me. People came in feeling awful and seeing no way forward. They left happy. And two weeks later, at the next appointment, all their old patterns were back.

“How do I help people change in a way that gives them lasting influence over it themselves?”

Since 1988 I have guided, trained and advised people through personal change. As a consultant, coach, hypnotherapist and Havening practitioner. Thousands of people. And I kept asking the same three questions. How does this work? What can I influence? And how can I use that influence to achieve what I want?

I asked those questions about my own life too. My own patterns became my first material for investigation. Whatever I discovered, I first applied to myself.

In 2014 I was at my lowest point. Ill, everything lost, no doctor who knew what to do. That’s when I decided: if no one can help me heal, I’ll do it myself. That’s where what would later be called ROOTS began.

The answer wasn’t in yet another layer of the story. It was in how neurobiology uses that story. Once I saw how it fits together, what had seemed complicated became manageable. And therefore something you can influence. That is how ROOTS grew.

The next step

Understanding is not the same as experiencing.

In the handbook and the masterclass I show you that it works this way. There is one thing I don’t show there: how you find the piece that sets off the alarm for you, and how that connection is broken.

Not because it’s a secret. Because it only works when you do it. With one pattern of your own. And with someone alongside you who sees what you can’t see in that moment.

That’s why there is the Trigger Breakfree Challenge.

From practice

What that looks like in real life

Sanne, 46, HR manager

Sanne knows her pattern inside out. She takes everything on. Years of therapy and piles of books have shown her where it came from. After her burnout, she genuinely changed things: fewer hours, boundaries, saying no. For months it goes well.

Then, during a meeting, there is a silence after someone asks who will take on a project. “I’ll do it.” She hears herself say it before she has decided.

A few weeks later, there is another silence. She notices it, but her body stays calm. She thinks: “I don’t have to do anything.” A colleague says, “I’ll do it.”

She is still someone who takes initiative. Now she does it when she chooses.

What it feels like

Nothing spectacular. Often perfectly ordinary. You find yourself in a situation that used to sweep you away, and notice that your ability to choose is still there. You feel what you feel, think what you think, and the old pattern does not take over.

That is where freedom begins: not because you have become better at controlling yourself, but because your survival program is no longer in the driver’s seat.

“But…”

Perhaps you’re thinking…

“I’ve already done so much.”

Exactly. Everything you have done worked with your story, your understanding or your behaviour. This works at the level underneath.

“Do I have to go back to my childhood again?”

No. We’re not looking for a new explanation. We’re looking for the piece your survival system uses. That’s something different from retelling your history.

“I’m too sensitive, too complex.”

A sensitive system reacts faster, yes. It also updates faster. Feeling a lot is no disadvantage here.

“Is this another quick promise?”

Honestly: it’s one week, with one pattern. You don’t tackle all your patterns at once. With one pattern, you experience what changes when you work at this level.
